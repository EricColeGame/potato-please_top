## How to Tell the Real Potato Please Official Trailer From a Fan Edit

Start by checking the channel, not the thumbnail. An authentic trailer is published by the verified studio or publisher account, and the description usually links to an official site, press kit, or store listing. Fan edits can look polished — sometimes impressively so — but they rarely have that paper trail.

The next thing to check is audio. Licensed music is credited somewhere, whether in the description or in the video itself. If the track is uncredited, or if the clip gets muted and re-uploaded a day later, that is a strong hint you are watching a copy rather than the source.

| Signal | Official trailer | Fan-made or reuploaded |
|---|---|---|
| Publishing account | Verified studio or publisher channel | Unverified uploader, generic name |
| Description | Links to official site, press kit, or store page | Vague text, shortened links |
| Audio credit | Licensed track or credited composer | Uncredited music, later muted |
| Branding | Consistent logo treatment and fonts | Cropped logos, visible borders |
| Comment section | Pinned note from the team | Disabled, or flooded with spam |
| Reuploads | Rare; takedowns are common | Multiple copies across channels |

### Red flags that suggest a fake clip

- A thumbnail that uses a different art style than the rest of the branding.
- A "leak" posted by an account created days earlier.
- Frame rates or aspect ratios that shift mid-video, which usually means spliced footage.
- Text overlays promising a specific date with no source attached.
- Comment sections where every question gets the same copy-pasted reply.

When in doubt, wait. A real announcement spreads across multiple community spaces within hours, and someone will always link the original upload. Patience beats a repost every time.
