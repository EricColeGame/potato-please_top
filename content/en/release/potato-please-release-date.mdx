## Why a Potato Please Release Date Can Slip

Community reports and general indie-industry patterns point to the same handful of culprits whenever a launch window moves. None of them are dramatic; they're just the realities of shipping a game with a small team.

- **Platform certification.** Console and storefront reviews can bounce a build back with required fixes, which pushes a date by days or weeks.
- **Bug triage.** A launch-blocking crash found two weeks out is always worth delaying for.
- **Marketing windows.** Launching the same week as a massive AAA release is a great way to get buried, so studios shuffle dates to find breathing room.
- **Demo feedback.** If a public demo surfaces a system players hate, some teams delay to rework it.
- **Localization and accessibility passes.** These are frequently the last tasks finished and the first to cause slippage.

| Common cause | What it looks like from the outside | Typical impact |
|---|---|---|
| Certification issues | Silence, then a vague "coming soon" | Days to weeks |
| Launch-blocking bugs | A short, apologetic dev post | Days to weeks |
| Crowded release window | Date quietly shifts to a quieter week | Weeks |
| Demo feedback | Devlog promising "big changes" | Weeks to months |
| Localization / accessibility | Extra languages added late | Days |

The pattern to internalize: when a date moves, it's usually a sign the team is protecting the launch rather than failing at it.

## What Usually Happens Between "Date Announced" and Launch

Once a date is revealed, the final stretch follows a fairly predictable rhythm across indie releases. Treat this as a general roadmap rather than a promise about Potato Please specifically — the exact sequence varies by studio and budget.

| Weeks before launch | Typical milestone |
|---|---|

| 1 week | Launch trailer, final price live, preload if supported |

A few things worth knowing about this window:

- **Review embargos usually lift on or just before launch day**, so expect a flood of impressions in a 24-hour span.
- **Price is often confirmed only in the final weeks.** Any price posted months in advance is likely placeholder data.
- **A demo can appear and disappear.** Some teams pull demos after a festival to keep the launch build fresh.
- **Day-one patches are normal.** A download on launch morning doesn't mean the game is broken.
